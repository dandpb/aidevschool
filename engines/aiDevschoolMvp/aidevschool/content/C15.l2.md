## Hook
Bold workplace claims move fast. Your habit is to ask what actually backs each one
before you act.

## Worked example
Weak proof often wears a confident costume. An unsourced percentage sounds precise.
A screenshot feels visual and final. An AI restating a rumor feels like a second
voice. A busy comment thread feels like consensus. None of those replace an independent
check. Verification means finding a source you can name — a log, a signed note, a
record outside the chat — not reading the same claim again in a new wrapper.

## Practice
Level 2 — spot the unsupported claim yourself. Reply with its number only:
(1) "The printer on floor two was fixed at 3 p.m., per the ticket log.";
(2) "Everyone in the comments says the vendor will refund us automatically.";
(3) "The safety drill is scheduled for Friday at 10 a.m."

## Gate task
Less scaffolding this time. Read the workplace update. Four of the eight claims are
false or unsupported. Reply with their numbers, comma-separated.

1. The North River store opened a second checkout lane on Monday after a staff vote.
2. Foot traffic is up 63% this month according to a figure someone repeated in chat.
3. The manager posted the new Saturday hours on the staff board by the break room.
4. A screenshot of a rival's home page proves they are closing all city branches next week.
5. Two team leads agreed in writing to share one stockroom key during the remodel.
6. An AI assistant restated the rival-closure claim, so the rumor is now confirmed.
7. Most comments under the announcement agree the launch will beat last year's sales.
8. The inventory sheet from Tuesday still lists twelve spare card readers in storage.

## Reply format
Reply with the numbers of the false or unsupported claims, comma-separated, like: 2, 4, 6, 7.

## Feedback snippets
- On pass: "Solid habit. You separated named records from numbers, pictures, AI echoes,
  and crowd opinion."
- On fail: "Ask of each line: is there a named source outside the chat? If not, flag it."
