## Hook
Headlines say AI will take every job — or change nothing at all. Which story should
you trust when you plan your own work?

## Worked example
Think of a job as a bundle of tasks, not one solid block. At Millfield City Council,
some form-filling is now faster with AI. New tasks appear too: checking drafts,
setting rules, and explaining results to the public. Most roles shift piece by piece.
They do not vanish overnight. Wider effects matter too. Think about who gains, who
falls behind, energy use, and new rules such as the EU AI Act. The useful response is
not panic and not blind trust. It is learning to work with AI carefully and critically.

## Practice
Level 1 — finish this line in your own words: "AI mostly changes jobs by changing
_____ , not by erasing whole roles overnight."

## Gate task
Your team is worried AI will change their jobs. What is the most balanced response
you can give?
A) "AI will replace everyone soon, so there is no point adapting."
B) "AI will change nothing; ignore it completely."
C) "Tasks will shift; let us learn to use AI well and keep human judgment in the loop."

Then, imagine a colleague asks you to explain it. Explain how AI is changing work and society to them in your own words, in 3 to 5 sentences.
## Reply format
Reply with one letter (A, B, or C) and one line on why.
## Feedback snippets
- On pass: "Balanced and useful — jobs shift task by task, extremes mislead, and the
  practical move is critical skill-building."
- On fail: "Avoid both extremes. AI changes tasks over time. The response is critical
  learning, not fear and not shrug."
