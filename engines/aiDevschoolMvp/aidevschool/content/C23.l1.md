## Hook
You send a client a polished plan. An AI wrote half of it. Do they deserve to know?

## Worked example
Transparency means saying AI helped when people will rely on the work. A short
plain note works: what the AI did, and what you checked. Hiding AI use can hurt
trust more than a clear line ever would. Disclosure does not move the blame to
the tool. You still own what you send. People who must decide or trust your work
deserve that clarity.

## Practice
Level 1 — finish this disclosure line: "Drafted with AI help; I checked ______."
Name one thing you would personally verify.

## Gate task
A colleague asks you to send the weekly summary you drafted with AI help, and your
manager will use it to decide the budget. What do you do?
A) Send it as-is and say nothing about the AI.
B) Send it with a short note that AI helped draft it and that you checked the figures.
C) Refuse to use AI at all for any work.

Then, imagine a colleague asks you to explain it. Explain why you should say when AI helped to them in your own words, in 3 to 5 sentences.
## Reply format
Reply with one letter (A, B, or C) and one line on why.
## Feedback snippets
- On pass: "Well done — a short honest note protects trust. Disclosure never hands
  your responsibility to the tool."
- On fail: "When trust or decisions are on the line, say AI helped and what you
  checked. Hiding it risks more than naming it."
