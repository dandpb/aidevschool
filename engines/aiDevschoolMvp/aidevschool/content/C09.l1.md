## Hook
A spam filter drops a real client email, then lets a scam through an hour later. How can
one tool make both kinds of mistake — and why can you not erase both at once?

## Worked example
Picture airport security. A false alarm is stopping a traveler who is fine. A missed
case is waving through a real risk. Stricter checks catch more real risks but bother
more innocent people. Looser checks do the opposite. A system that sorts cases into
labels like "spam" or "not spam" faces the same trade-off. It learns patterns, not
perfect truth, so both error types appear. Teams choose a balance based on what each
mistake costs. No setting removes every error. Important flags and important misses still
need a person's judgment.

## Practice
Level 1 — match the mistake. Reply with two labels: (1) a healthy patient told "you may
be sick"; (2) a sick patient told "you are fine." Use the words **false alarm** and
**missed case**.

## Feedback snippets

  habit. That is how grown-up teams live with imperfect sorters."
