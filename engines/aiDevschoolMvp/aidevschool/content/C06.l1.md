## Hook
A team builds a hiring tool by feeding it old résumés. Later it prefers one group of people.
Where did the unfairness come from — the code, or the examples it practiced on?

## Worked example
Imagine teaching a friend to sort apples by color using a basket of fruit. If half the
"red" labels are wrong, your friend will sort badly no matter how carefully they try.
An AI works the same way. During the AI's practice phase, when it learns from examples,
it copies patterns in the data you give it — including mistakes, gaps, and old bias.
Clean, fair, complete examples lead to better know-how. Messy or skewed examples lead
to messy or skewed answers. People say "garbage in, garbage out" for this reason. The
data is not a side detail. It is the main ingredient.

## Practice
Level 1 — pick the better data set with me. A shop wants an AI to flag broken products
from photos. Reply **A** or **B**, then one short reason: (A) only perfect product shots
from one camera; (B) many angles, lighting, and both broken and fine items, labeled by
staff who checked each one.

## Gate task
The tutor will send you three short questions, one at a time or together. Answer each
with A, B, or C.
## Reply format
Reply with just the three letters in order, like: A C B.
## Feedback snippets
- On pass: "Yes — the examples are the ingredient list. Fix the data, or the know-how
  will keep tasting like the mistakes."
- On fail: "Ask what the AI practiced on. Wrong, thin, or skewed examples almost always
  show up later as wrong, thin, or skewed answers."
