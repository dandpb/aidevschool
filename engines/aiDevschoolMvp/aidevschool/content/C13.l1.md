## Hook
Your first instructions to an AI were unclear. The reply was fluffy. What is the
smallest change that would help on the next try?

## Worked example
Maya types: "Say something about our office move." She gets a long, vague essay. She
does not start over from zero. She keeps the same job and upgrades the instructions.
She adds who it is for, a calm tone, and a four-bullet limit. The second reply is
usable. Iterating means improving the instructions you type for the AI (your prompt)
— not answering the weak ask yourself. Add context, an explicit format, an example, or
a clear limit. Then paste the improved brief, not a meta-comment about prompting.

## Practice
Level 1 — upgrade this weak line with me. Fill the brackets, then send all three lines:
Task: draft a short note about the [event] for the team.
Context: the move happens next [day]; desks stay the same.
Format: [how many] short bullets, calm tone.

## Gate task
Final task — no help this time. Rewrite this prompt so it has all three parts:
'Give me some tips for the team offsite.'

Then, imagine a colleague asks you to explain it. Explain why the AI sounds so sure of itself to them in your own words, in 3 to 5 sentences.
## Reply format
Reply with exactly three lines, in this order: Task: / Context: / Format: — one line each.
## Feedback snippets
- On pass: "Nice upgrade — same job, sharper instructions, ready to paste. That is how
  iteration works."
- On fail: "Do not settle for the vague reply. Rewrite the instruction as Task, Context,
  and Format, one line each, then try again."
