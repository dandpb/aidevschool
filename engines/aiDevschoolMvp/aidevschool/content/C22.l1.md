## Hook
A teammate opens a chatbot to "save time" on client work. What safe steps should come
before and after that box?

## Worked example
Priya at Harborline Logistics needs a polite status update for a shipping delay. She
first strips names, account numbers, and addresses. She pastes only a clean outline.
She asks the AI for a draft. Then she checks every fact against her own notes. She
asks her lead to approve before it goes out. That is a safe workplace loop: clean
inputs, clear task, human review, and a named owner. AI drafts. People still decide.

## Practice
Level 1 — order these three steps for a client email draft: (1) manager approval,
(2) remove personal details, (3) ask AI for a draft. Reply with the numbers in order.

## Gate task
You work at Blue Orchard Bank. Your manager wants a short FAQ page about a new savings
product for the public website. You have a one-page fact sheet with approved wording.
A junior colleague wants real customer complaint emails in the chatbot for "tone."
Then they want the AI text posted live with no review.

What should you do?

A) Paste the real complaint emails so the tone feels authentic, then publish.
B) Generate the FAQ with AI from anything handy and skip review to meet the deadline.
C) Use only the approved fact sheet with no personal details. Edit the draft. Get
manager approval before publishing.

Reply with the letter of your choice and one line on why.

## Reply format
Reply with the letter of your choice and one line on why. Example: C — only approved
non-personal facts go in; a person reviews and approves before publish.

## Feedback snippets
- On pass: "That's the safe loop — clean inputs, AI draft, human edit, named approval.
  Real customer data stays out."
- On fail: "Never paste real customer details. Start from approved facts, check the
  draft, and get approval before anything goes live."
