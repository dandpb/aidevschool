## Hook
Your queue is busy. Someone proposes "just let the AI handle approvals end to end."
What breaks if no person owns the outcome?

## Worked example
Speed without ownership is risk. When a choice can harm money, access, safety, or
trust, a human must check and stay answerable. The AI can prepare options. It
cannot hold duty. Good oversight is active: you know what was checked, what was
uncertain, and who signed off.

## Practice
Level 2 — write your own one-line rule for when AI output may go straight to a
customer, and when a person must review first.

## Gate task
Workplace decision. Your team uses an AI that scores loan-style payment plans for
small business clients. Today it marks client "Harbor Supplies" as high risk and
recommends a hard stop on their plan. The score is a short note with no sources.
A junior colleague wants to auto-send the stop notice to save time. The client
relationship and cash flow both matter.

Which action is best?
A) A named person reviews the file and evidence, then decides and owns the outcome
B) Let the AI send the stop notice on its own so no one slows the queue
C) Hide the AI score and guess, so nobody can question the tool later

Reply with the letter of your choice and one line on why.

## Reply format
Reply with one letter (A, B, or C) and one line on why, like: A — a person must review and own the call.

## Feedback snippets
- On pass: "Yes — accountability stays with people. Tools assist; they do not
  replace ownership."
- On fail: "Name a human owner for the decision. Suggestions can be automatic;
  responsibility cannot."
