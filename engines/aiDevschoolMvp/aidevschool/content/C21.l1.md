## Hook
An AI flags a customer as "high risk" and blocks their order. Who is accountable
if that call was wrong?

## Worked example
AI can draft, rank, and suggest at speed. It does not take the blame. A person
must review high-impact choices, and a person stays answerable for what goes out.
Think of the AI as a fast junior helper: useful notes, final pen in your hand.
Human oversight means you read the stakes, check the weak spots, and own the
decision. Handing the whole choice to the tool is not responsible use.

## Practice
Level 1 — pick with support. A chatbot drafts a refund denial. Safest next step?
A) Send it as-is in one click
B) Skim for tone only and send
C) A person checks the facts and policy, then decides
The strong answer is C — say why in a few words.

## Gate task
Workplace decision. Your team uses an AI that scores loan-style payment plans for
small business clients. Today it marks client "Harbor Supplies" as high risk and
recommends a hard stop on their plan. The score is a short note with no sources.
A junior colleague wants to auto-send the stop notice to save time. The client
relationship and cash flow both matter.

Which action is best?
A) A named person reviews the file and evidence, then decides and owns the outcome
B) Let the AI send the stop notice on its own so no one slows the queue
C) Hide the AI score and guess, so nobody can question the tool later

Reply with the letter of your choice and one line on why.

## Reply format
Reply with one letter (A, B, or C) and one line on why, like: A — a person must review and own the call.

## Feedback snippets
- On pass: "Exactly — the helper can suggest, but a person reviews and stays
  accountable. Oversight is part of the job, not a delay."
- On fail: "High-impact calls need a human owner. The AI may assist; it does not
  decide or carry the blame."
