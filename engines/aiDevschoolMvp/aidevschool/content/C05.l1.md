## Hook
An AI says: "I'm 90% sure this email is spam." The email turns out to be real. Was the
AI lying to you? What does the 90% actually mean?

## Worked example
A weather app says 70% chance of rain, and the day stays dry. That is not a lie — it
means about 3 days in 10 like this one would stay dry. The number is an estimate of how
often the system expects to be right in similar cases, drawn from patterns in data. The
same is true for the AI's 90%: it is a confidence estimate, not a guarantee and not a
count of how much it checked. Some uncertainty always remains, because it works from
patterns, not from certainty.

## Practice
Level 1 — we read two everyday numbers together. For each, reply "right" or "wrong"
about what the percentage means: (1) a spam filter scoring an email 95% spam; (2) a map
app saying the trip "usually takes 20 minutes".

## Gate task
The tutor will send you three short questions, one at a time or together. Answer each
with A, B, or C.

Then, imagine a colleague asks you to explain it. Explain what an AI's percentage really means to them in your own words, in 3 to 5 sentences.
## Reply format
Reply with just the three letters in order, like: A C B.

## Feedback snippets
- On pass: "Right — a percentage is the AI's estimate of how often it is right in similar
  cases, never a promise. A single surprising outcome does not make it wrong."
- On fail: "Ask what the number counts: similar cases the system would be right in — not
  proof, and not how much it checked."
