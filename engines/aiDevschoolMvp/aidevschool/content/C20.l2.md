## Hook
AI can draft fast. Speed alone is not a reason to hand it every task. How do you
decide?

## Worked example
At Northwind Supplies, Leo uses AI for a late-invoice reminder. The task is clear and
easy to check, so AI helps. The same day he must rank job candidates. That choice
changes lives, so he keeps it. Good judgment means matching the tool to the stakes,
not to the clock.

## Practice
Level 2 — decide alone. A public product blurb needs a rewrite. You can check every
line. Reply with A, B, or C and one short reason.
A) skip AI. B) draft with AI then edit. C) send AI text unchecked.

## Gate task
At Cedar Ridge Clinic you must write a short internal note about a new shift rota.
The facts are already agreed. The note only restates them in plain words. A colleague
wants AI to rank three job candidates from their CVs. They want that ranking emailed
to the hiring panel with no human review.

What should you do?

A) Use AI for both tasks without checking, because AI is always faster.
B) Draft the rota note with AI and check it. Keep candidate ranking as your human task.
C) Use AI for everything on your plate today, including the ranking email.

Reply with the letter of your choice and one line on why.

## Reply format
Reply with the letter of your choice and one line on why.

## Feedback snippets
- On pass: "Solid judgment — AI for checkable low-stakes drafts; human ownership for
  high-stakes calls."
- On fail: "Stakes and ownership decide, not speed. If harm is hard to undo, you keep
  the work."
