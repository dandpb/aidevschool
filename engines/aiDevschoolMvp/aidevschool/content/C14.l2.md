## Hook
A polished AI summary lands in your report. Days later, three "facts" fall apart.
How can wording that feels so sure still invent things?

## Worked example
An AI can write a smooth life story without checking a single fact. It predicts the
next word from patterns it learned in its practice phase. Names, dates, and awards
often sound right because those patterns reward fluent phrasing. When a detail is
missing, it still fills the gap with something that fits. That is when the AI
confidently makes something up. Smooth tone is not a check against a source.

## Practice
Level 2 — find the made-up claim on your own. Reply with its number only:
(1) "Water freezes at 0°C."; (2) "The Amazon River is the longest river on Mars.";
(3) "A year has twelve months."

## Gate task
Your turn with less help. An AI wrote the biography below. Four of the nine claims
are planted fabrications. Reply with the numbers of the false claims, comma-separated.

1. Grace Hopper was an American computer scientist and a rear admiral in the U.S. Navy.
2. She was born in London in 1906 and emigrated to New York as a child.
3. During World War II she worked on the Harvard Mark I computer.
4. Her team popularized the term "computer bug" after finding a moth stuck in a relay.
5. She single-handedly invented the COBOL programming language in 1952.
6. She argued that programs should be written in English-like words, not only in mathematical notation.
7. The Navy named a destroyer, USS Hopper, after her in 1971.
8. She retired as one of the oldest officers on active duty.
9. She received the Nobel Prize in Computer Science in 1986.

## Reply format
Reply with the numbers of the false claims, comma-separated, like: 2, 5, 7, 9.

## Feedback snippets
- On pass: "Good eye — fluent wording is not proof. You marked the claims that need a
  real check before anyone relies on them."
- On fail: "Scan again for birthplaces, invention stories, ship dates, and prizes. Any
  claim the AI could have filled in is worth a second look."
