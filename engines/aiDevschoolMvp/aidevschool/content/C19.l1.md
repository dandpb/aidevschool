## Hook
A sleek post claims a big new rule starts tomorrow. It looks professional. Do you
forward it, or do you check first?

## Worked example
Jordan sees a clean article saying a city will ban cash next month. The design looks
serious. An AI summary repeats the claim in calm words. That still is not evidence.
Smooth writing — from a person or an AI — does not prove the facts. The useful move is
to leave the page. Look for an independent, trustworthy source. Check who is behind the
claim, whether others report it, and whether a date or number can be verified. Before
you forward a surprising claim, verify it first.

## Practice
Level 1 — pick the safer next step. A post says "New tax starts Monday." Reply with
the letter: A) Forward it so friends are ready. B) Leave the post and check a trusted
news or government source. C) Trust it because the layout looks official.

## Gate task
The tutor will send you three short questions, one at a time or together. Answer each
with A, B, or C.

Then, imagine a colleague asks you to explain it. Explain how to judge whether a source is trustworthy to them in your own words, in 3 to 5 sentences.
## Reply format
Reply with just the three letters in order, like: A C B.
## Feedback snippets
- On pass: "Strong work. Confident text is not proof, and you leave the page to check
  who is behind a claim before you rely on it."
- On fail: "Remember: smooth writing proves nothing. Leave the page, check a trustworthy
  source, and verify before you forward."
