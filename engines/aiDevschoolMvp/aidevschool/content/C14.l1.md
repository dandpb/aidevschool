## Hook
A colleague pastes an AI's confident summary into a report. Later you find three of
its facts were made up. How can a system that sounds so sure still invent things?

## Worked example
Maya asks an AI to write a short biography of a famous computer scientist. The result
reads fluently — names, dates, an award. It sounds authoritative. But two claims are
wrong: a birthplace, and a prize that does not exist. The AI did not look anything up.
It produced text that *sounds* like a biography, predicting each next word from
patterns in what it read during practice. Smooth, confident phrasing is exactly what
it was trained to produce — even when the facts are missing. A confident sentence is
not a checked fact.

## Practice
Level 1 — let's spot one together. Below, one claim is fabricated. Reply with its
number: (1) "The Eiffel Tower is in Paris."; (2) "The Eiffel Tower was built in 1492
to celebrate a sea voyage."; (3) "It is made mostly of iron."

## Gate task
Check time. A short biography an AI assistant wrote is below. Four of the nine claims
are planted fabrications. Reply with the numbers of the false claims, comma-separated.

1. Grace Hopper was an American computer scientist and a rear admiral in the U.S. Navy.
2. She was born in London in 1906 and emigrated to New York as a child.
3. During World War II she worked on the Harvard Mark I computer.
4. Her team popularized the term "computer bug" after finding a moth stuck in a relay.
5. She single-handedly invented the COBOL programming language in 1952.
6. She argued that programs should be written in English-like words, not only in mathematical notation.
7. The Navy named a destroyer, USS Hopper, after her in 1971.
8. She retired as one of the oldest officers on active duty.
9. She received the Nobel Prize in Computer Science in 1986.

## Reply format
Reply with the numbers of the false claims, comma-separated, like: 2, 5, 7, 9.

## Feedback snippets
- On pass: "Exactly — fluent and confident does not mean checked. When the stakes are
  real, you confirm the specific claims before you rely on them."
- On fail: "Find every claim that names a date, a place, or an award it could be making
  up. Confidence is not evidence."
