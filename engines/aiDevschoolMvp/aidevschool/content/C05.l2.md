## Hook
An AI posts a high confidence score and still misses. If the number was not a lie,
what was it?

## Worked example
Confidence is an estimate of how often similar cases go a certain way — not a promise
about this one case, and not a count of checks performed. Patterns leave leftover
uncertainty. One surprise does not prove bad faith; it reminds you to keep a margin.

## Practice
Level 2 — in two sentences, explain to a colleague why "85% likely" on a label can be
honest and still wrong today. No bullet list.

## Gate task
The tutor will send you three short questions, one at a time or together. Answer each
with A, B, or C.

Then, imagine a colleague asks you to explain it. Explain what an AI's percentage really means to them in your own words, in 3 to 5 sentences.
## Reply format
Reply with just the three letters in order, like: A C B.
## Feedback snippets
- On pass: "Right — a percentage is an estimate about similar cases, never a promise.
  Uncertainty stays, so you still judge the stakes."
- On fail: "Read the number as 'how often in similar cases,' not as proof and not as
  how hard it checked. One miss can still fit an honest estimate."
