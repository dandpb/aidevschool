## Hook
Two teams use the same AI product. One gets solid results. The other gets nonsense.
Same tool — different examples in the practice phase. What should you inspect first?

## Worked example
An AI only repeats patterns found in its examples. If labels are wrong, groups are
missing, or old bias sits in the records, those problems become part of the know-how
it learned. Bigger is not automatically better. Fair, complete, well-checked data beats
a giant messy pile. When results look off, start with the ingredient list — the data —
before blaming the interface.

## Practice
Level 2 — on your own. Name one real workplace risk if a loan tool practiced only on
applications from one age group. One or two sentences.

## Gate task
The tutor will send you three short questions, one at a time or together. Answer each
with A, B, or C.
## Reply format
Reply with just the three letters in order, like: A C B.
## Feedback snippets
- On pass: "Sharp — you traced bad outputs back to bad inputs. That habit will save you
  from trusting shiny tools fed on shaky examples."
- On fail: "When results fail, audit the examples first: labels, missing groups, and
  whether practice cases match real life."
