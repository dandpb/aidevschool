## Hook
Your boss wants every email drafted by AI from now on. When does that help, and when
should you keep the work yourself?

## Worked example
Leo works at Northwind Supplies. He needs a friendly reminder email about a late
invoice. AI is a good fit: low risk, easy to check, and he keeps the final say. Later
he faces a hiring shortlist. That choice affects real people and needs judgment he
cannot outsource. The rule is simple. Use AI when the task is clear, the stakes are
low or medium, and you can check the result. Keep the work yourself when the stakes
are high, the facts are thin, or only you can own the call.

## Gate task
Decision time at Cedar Ridge Clinic. You must write a short internal note about a new
shift rota for next month. The facts are already agreed. The note only restates them
in plain words. Separately, a colleague wants AI to rank three job candidates from
their CVs. They want that ranking emailed to the hiring panel with no human review.

What should you do?

A) Use AI for both tasks without checking, because AI is always faster.
B) Draft the rota note with AI and check it. Keep candidate ranking as your human task.
C) Use AI for everything on your plate today, including the ranking email.

Reply with the letter of your choice and one line on why.

## Reply format
Reply with the letter of your choice and one line on why. Example: B — low-stakes
notes can use AI with a check; hiring ranks need a human owner.

## Feedback snippets
- On pass: "Yes — match the tool to the stakes. Draft low-risk text with AI and check
  it. Keep high-stakes calls under human judgment."
- On fail: "Ask two questions: can I check the result, and who owns the harm if it is
  wrong? High-stakes choices stay with you."
