## Hook
Misinformation often arrives dressed as careful reporting. Your filter has to work
even when the design looks perfect.

## Worked example
A confident page is still just a page. An AI restating it is still the same claim.
Good evaluation means stepping outside that loop. You ask who published it, whether
independent sources agree, and whether a concrete date or number checks out. Popularity,
polish, and calm tone do not replace those checks. The practical rule stays simple:
before you forward or act on a surprising claim, verify it first.

## Practice
Level 2 — choose the best check with less help. A polished newsletter says a major
brand "will vanish next week." Reply with the letter of the strongest move:
A) Quote the newsletter in your team chat as breaking news.
B) Find an independent, trustworthy report that confirms or denies the claim.
C) Decide based on how many emoji reactions the newsletter got.

## Gate task
The tutor will send you three short questions, one at a time or together. Answer each
with A, B, or C.

Then, imagine a colleague asks you to explain it. Explain how to judge whether a source is trustworthy to them in your own words, in 3 to 5 sentences.
## Reply format
Reply with just the three letters in order, like: A C B.
## Feedback snippets
- On pass: "Exactly — you judge sources by independence and evidence, not by polish or
  confidence."
- On fail: "Return to the core moves: leave the page, check who is behind the claim, and
  verify before you forward."
